# Writing the posts

Read this file whenever you write a post text.

## X

- At most 280 characters. Every link counts as 23, however long. The user may add a take line on top on Today, so aim for 220 or less.
- One idea. The first line must make people stop: a clear claim, a number from the source, or a short story start. Not a question like "Did you know…?".
- Short lines. A blank line between parts is fine.
- No hashtags. No emoji, or one if the user's samples use them.
- News: end with the source link. Product: the product link only when the post is about the product.
- Links: use the article's own link and the space's product link. Never copy a link from old posts in `get_recent_posts`: those are the app's click-counting links.

## LinkedIn

- About 600 to 1,200 characters (3,000 is the hard limit). Shorter is fine when the true facts run out: never pad.
- First line: a short hook that makes sense alone, because LinkedIn shows only the first two lines before "see more".
- Short paragraphs of one to three lines, with a blank line between them. A short list of three points works well.
- End with a plain line: a question to readers, what the user will do next, or the link.
- At most 2 hashtags, at the very end, and only if the user's samples use them.
- News: the source link in the text. Product: the product link once, near the end, only when it fits.

## Both

- Write the X and LinkedIn texts as two different posts on the same idea, each made for its place. Not the same text cut short.
- Use the user's voice from the space's `styleSamples`: their sentence length, their words, how formal they are. With no samples: plain, first person, short sentences, no hashtags, no emoji, and say once at the end of the run that pasting 2 or 3 old posts on the Space page makes drafts sound more like them.
- The space's `tone`: PLAIN = short, clear sentences, no hype; FRIENDLY = warm, a bit personal, often a question to readers at the end; BOLD = a strong first line and a clear opinion (still true, never rude). The tone always applies; the "no samples" default above only sets the words (first person, no hashtags, no emoji). When there are samples and they disagree with the tone, the samples win.
- Never: made-up facts or numbers, "As an AI", hype words ("game-changer", "revolutionary", "unleash", "dive in", "in today's fast-paced world"), clickbait, tagging people, asking for likes or follows.
- Write about the user's own work in the first person ("I", "we" if they have a team).
- Product mentions say what it does for the reader in plain words, never like an ad ("NorthList shows only your job mails"), not "Check out NorthList, the best…".
- Before sending, read each text once as the user's follower would: is it true, clear, and worth a minute?
